    ADR-149: Reset and top-up are in-place, rf-locked passes over every shard¶

    Status: Proposed Date: 2026-10-09 Issue: #470 Amends: ADR-145 (I1, I7, I8: a top-up is an operator grant above the plan) Related: ADR-125, ADR-137, ADR-139, ADR-140, ADR-141, ADR-145, ADR-147, #471, #674

    Context¶

    The #674 use case needs a plan upgrade, a purchase of more allowance, or a session reset to take effect immediately for one (entity, resource). Today nothing does that: set_limits() changes a bucket's parameters but never adds tokens, so an upgraded quota (which does not drip, ADR-137) keeps its old remaining balance until the next reset, and a purchase has no representation at all.

    PR #471 resets by deleting the bucket items. That loses the consumption counter the aggregator diffs (#179), the disabled stamp, shard_count and the ADR-145 grant record, and an in-flight adjust() (an unconditional ADD) recreates a skeleton item with no parameters.

    Three facts about the current code constrain an in-place write. Every refill clamps a balance to its ceiling, which for a quota is C // gc, so a credit above the ceiling is trimmed by the next pass of any writer. rf is one attribute per item, so a write that moves it without materialising every limit on the item skips their refill and any pending reset edge. And a sharded quota holds its allowance in slices whose sum ADR-145 conserves, so a credit to one shard is a mint unless it is counted.

    Decision¶

    Reset and top-up of an (entity, resource) must be in-place, rf-locked passes over every existing shard in one transaction, recorded by one operation record per call. A quota may be topped up above its ceiling only through b_{q}_tu, which ends with the period and is gated at 0.17.0.

    Consequences¶

    Positive: - Plan upgrades, purchases and session resets take effect on the next request, on every shard. - Each shard write is a zero-consumption slow-path pass: every limit materialised, the named ones changed by a token delta (never a set), tc, disabled and the parent untouched, and the shard's next acquire forced through one materialising pass. Nothing the item carries is lost; the fast path stays 0 RCU + 1 WCU and reads nothing new. - The operation record (put only if absent, in the same transaction) lets exactly one write per id commit and resolves an unclear failure; a caller may supply the id as an idempotency key. - A reset starts a new period: a quota's grant is recorded at the planned count; a session window is marked ended and applied, its balance left for the next opener to restore. - A quota top-up adds exactly N. A dripping limit's is capped at its ceiling: it has no period to bound extra headroom, so raising its rate is set_limits' job. - ADR-145 is amended, not replaced: a top-up is an operator grant above the plan, so I1 gains it as a source, I7's ceiling becomes C // gc + tu, and I8 reads admitted plus held plus still-grantable equals the capacity plus the top-ups granted. - The aggregator and concurrent slow paths are excluded by the rf lock they already honour; older clients and aggregators, which would clamp tu away, by the ADR-141 gate. - Imperative only (no manifest or CloudFormation surface); audited; this process's rejection cache (ADR-147) is cleared. Details and costs: docs/plans/2026-10-09-reset-and-top-up-design.md.

    Negative: - Cost is O(shards) reads and two write units per shard, plus two for the operation record and two for the audit event, which ride in the same transaction so a grant and its audit commit together: even a one-shard operation is a three-item transaction (6 WRU where one UpdateItem and a separate audit PutItem were 2), against one delete per shard in #471. - Without an idempotency key, an error from a reset or top-up leaves its outcome unknown to the caller; the operation record lives three days. - A record is tied to the entity's incarnation (#716's pinc), read before the write rather than checked inside it: an entity deleted and recreated during one call can see a key retry applied once more. Closing it would put a ConditionCheck on META in every reset and top-up (+2 WCU a call); v0.17.0 grants dynamodb:ConditionCheckItem (ADR-150), but its check also pins the parent generation, so every set_parent would cancel resets in flight. Left open. A top-up that creates the bucket already carries ADR-150's check. - After a plan upgrade a quota's ceiling is at most one top-up wider than the entitlement until the next reset; it under-clamps refunds, never mints. - A conflicting writer on any shard aborts the whole transaction; the operation retries a bounded number of times and then fails. - A topped-up balance can sit on shards a request does not draw, so a draw can reject while the entity holds purchased tokens elsewhere, as ADR-145 already accepts for quotas. - Another process's rejection cache can refuse for up to its TTL after a reset elsewhere. - Reset and top-up are operator actions by contract. IAM cannot stop a principal that may acquire from writing its own buckets, so an application can grant itself allowance; a policy can only keep it out of the library's calls, not off the items. - Every reset and roll writer, client and aggregator, gains one attribute to clear, and ADR-145's conservation fuzz test and writer registry gain a term. - A credit landing between the read and the write can lift a balance above its target until the forced materialising pass clamps it.

    Alternatives Considered¶

    • Delete the bucket items (#471): loses the consumption counter, disabled, shard_count and the grant record, and in-flight adjustments recreate a corrupt item.
    • Set the balance without materialising the item: moving rf skips other limits' refill and pending reset edges; not moving it lets a stale aggregator refill land on top.
    • Top-up capped at the ceiling only: cannot express a purchase beyond the plan.
    • Purchase as a raised configured capacity: persists into later periods and needs a second admin write to undo.
    • Independent per-shard writes: a partial reset leaves overlapping quota grants, which over-admits.
